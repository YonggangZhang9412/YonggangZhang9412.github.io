Biography

My research focuses on trustworthy AI under deployment shift—the gap between development-time assumptions and real-world requirements. I study how AI systems can adapt to changing conditions, verify whether adaptations lead to genuine improvements, and preserve existing capabilities. My work spans robust machine learning, reliable adaptation of foundation models, and the self-evolution of autonomous agents.

My current research aims to establish the theoretical and algorithmic foundations of reliable agent self-evolution, enabling autonomous agents to acquire reusable expertise and improve through deployment experience while remaining reliable and accountable to evolving human goals and permissions.

I am currently a Research Assistant Professor at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST). Previously, I was a Postdoctoral Fellow at HKUST, working with Prof. Yike Guo (Fellow of IEEE and the Royal Academy of Engineering), and at Hong Kong Baptist University (HKBU), working with Prof. Yiu-ming Cheung (IEEE Fellow) and Prof. Bo Han. I received my Ph.D. from the University of Science and Technology of China (USTC) in 2022, advised by Prof. Xinmei Tian.

Research Interests

My research explores how AI systems can reliably adapt and improve under evolving real-world conditions, with three interconnected directions:

  • Reliable Self-Evolution of Autonomous Agents: learning from deployment experience, harness-level adaptation, verifiable self-improvement, and governable agent systems.
  • Reliable Adaptation of Foundation Models: targeted model adaptation, reasoning and alignment, and learning from reliable feedback.
  • Robust Machine Learning under Distribution Shift: adversarial robustness, out-of-distribution generalization and detection, and causal approaches to model reliability.

Selected Research

Selected work illustrating the development of my research from robust learning to reliable foundation models and autonomous agents. For a complete list, see the Publications page.

Verifiable Self-Evolution of Autonomous Agents

  • TTHE: Test-Time Harness Evolution (arXiv, 2026).
    Evolving agent harnesses from unlabeled execution traces while keeping model weights fixed.
  • EvolveNet: Collaborative Harness Evolution for Agent Self-Improvement (arXiv, 2026).
    Combining improvements from independent deployment environments into a shared agent harness.
  • A Theory of Reliable Self-Evolution for Agent Harnesses (arXiv, 2026).
    Studying when harness modifications can be reliably adopted and how verification limits self-improvement.

Reliable Adaptation of Foundation Models

  • Interpreting and Improving Large Language Models in Arithmetic Calculation (ICML 2024, Oral).
    Identifying critical model components for targeted improvements in arithmetic reasoning.
  • Enhancing LLMs for Knowledge Base Question Answering by Chain-of-Decomposition (ICLR 2026).
    Decomposing knowledge-base question answering to enable targeted adaptation.
  • Conditional Equivalence of DPO and RLHF: Implicit Assumption, Failure Modes, and Provable Alignment (ICML 2026, Spotlight).
    Examining when preference optimization is aligned with intended preferences.

Robust Machine Learning

  • CausalAdv: Adversarial Robustness through the Lens of Causality (ICLR 2022).
    Understanding adversarial vulnerability through causal data-generation mechanisms.
  • Detecting Generated Images by Fitting Natural Image Distributions (NeurIPS 2025, Spotlight).
    Detecting generated images through properties of natural-image distributions.
